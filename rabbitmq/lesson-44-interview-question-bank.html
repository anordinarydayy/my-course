44Course finale · Patterns & interview

The Interview Question Bank

Forty-three lessons in, the interview questions themselves stop being surprising — durable versus persistent, quorum versus classic, does RabbitMQ do exactly-once. What separates a passable answer from a strong one is never the headline claim; it's whether you can state the boundary the interviewer is about to ask about before they ask it.

Original study chapter — course finale. The question bank, answer register, and mock exchange are written from scratch, synthesizing all nine phases of this course. Every version claim targets open-source RabbitMQ 4.3, with the signed 4.3.3 maintenance release as the current patch overlay (public release/download/docs pages observed at 4.3.2 on the 2026-07-21 access date). This chapter contains three original figures, two interactive.
00 Framing

How interviewers actually probe

A strong candidate says “durable queue plus persistent message means the metadata and the message both survive a restart.” A strong interviewer's next question is always the boundary: “does that mean no message loss?” The candidate who has internalized this course says no, cites the confirm and the routing return as the other two guarantees that have to be layered on, and names the failure mode that still gets through. The candidate who stops at the headline claim gets a pushback question they weren't ready for.

This lesson is organized around that dynamic: a table of current, defensible answers with their boundary stated explicitly; a register of answers that were once correct and now quietly fail; and a table of exact wording to avoid, paired with the wording that survives a follow-up question.

The core distinction

Every answer in this lesson has two parts: the defensible claim and the boundary that must be stated. Give only the first half and you sound confident until the follow-up question; give both and the follow-up question is the one you already answered.

01 Reference

Concise current answers ▸

Interview questionDefensible 4.3 answerBoundary that must be stated
Durable queue vs. persistent message?Durable describes queue metadata surviving a restart; persistent is a message delivery mode relevant to durable storage. Use both where restart survival matters, plus confirms and the right queue type.Neither alone proves routing, replication, consumer processing, or unconditional no loss.
Consumer ack vs. publisher confirm?Orthogonal, in opposite directions: consumer ack covers broker-to-consumer responsibility; confirm covers publisher-to-broker acceptance.Neither is an end-to-end business receipt.
Does RabbitMQ give exactly-once?Confirms and acks alone provide no atomic transaction with a database or API. Reliable designs accept duplicates and make effects idempotent.State the hop and mechanism; avoid the broader unsourced slogan "exactly once is impossible everywhere."
Quorum vs. classic queue?Quorum is replicated, always durable, consensus-based — preferred for important data needing HA. Classic is non-replicated and can fit temporary/high-churn cases quorum doesn't suit.Quorum is not best for every queue; classic remains the default type.
Is queue declaration idempotent?Redeclaring with equivalent properties is accepted.A property mismatch causes a channel-level 406 PRECONDITION_FAILED; use policies for mutable settings.
What do confirms guarantee?RabbitMQ confirms once routed queues reach their documented acceptance boundary; quorum confirms follow quorum acceptance.An unroutable publication may still be confirmed; confirms don't prove consumer processing.
How do you prevent poison-message loops?Classify errors, bound attempts and age, delay retries, park exhausted work, replay idempotently. 4.3 quorum queues can use native delayed retry.basic.nack returns do not advance quorum delivery-count — the default limit is not a universal loop breaker.
Direct Reply-To or callback queue?Direct Reply-To avoids real reply queues but is at most once; a callback queue can buffer per queue semantics.Choose by reply durability and requester-disconnect behavior, not a performance slogan.
Does RabbitMQ preserve order?A queue has an ordering discipline, but competing consumers, redelivery, priorities, and retries can change observed completion order.Name the required scope; use per-key routing or Single Active Consumer only when the trade-off fits.
When should RabbitMQ not be the default?When the dominant need is long retained replay, very large fan-out better served by a log, or an existing cloud-native/Redis workflow that's already acceptable.RabbitMQ Streams may satisfy retained-log needs — compare the actual workload, not a product category.
What metadata store does 4.3 use?Khepri is the only supported store; Mnesia was removed.The canonical metadata landing page still contains stale contradictory text — release notes govern.
Does RabbitMQ need an AMQP 1.0 plugin?No. Native AMQP 1.0 has been core since RabbitMQ 4.0.Exactly-once link recovery and AMQP transactions remain unsupported; address v1 is deprecated.
02 Register

Outdated or incomplete answers that quietly fail you

Outdated answerCurrent correction
"Use mirrored classic queues for HA."Removed in 4.0. Use quorum queues or streams when replicated safety is required.
"Mnesia remains selectable in RabbitMQ 4.3."The signed 4.3.0 release and reworked partition guide say Mnesia was removed; the metadata landing page's contradictory text is stale.
"Every requeue reaches the default quorum delivery limit of 20."basic.nack returns do not advance delivery-count in 4.3 — explicitly bound retries at the application level.
"DLX + TTL is RabbitMQ's only delayed retry."4.3 quorum queues also have native capped-linear delayed retry.
"Quorum queues do not support priorities."RabbitMQ 4.3 added strict priorities for quorum queues.
"Transient non-exclusive queues are normal temporary queues."Deprecated and disabled by default in 4.3.
"Durable + persistent guarantees no loss."Add confirms, routing handling, matching queue type, and client recovery — the unresolved-confirm duplicate window remains.
"Acks and confirms are the same."Orthogonal mechanisms on different hops of the message's path.
"RabbitMQ gives exactly-once business effects."Broker mechanisms don't make arbitrary external side effects atomic — design for duplicates and idempotency.
"RabbitMQ defaults every queue to quorum."Classic remains the default type unless configuration changes it.
"AMQP 1.0 requires rabbitmq_amqp1_0."Native AMQP 1.0 has been core since 4.0.
"RabbitMQ only has destructive queues; Kafka alone has logs."RabbitMQ Streams are retained, replicated, replayable logs.
"Kafka consumers can only share work by whole-partition assignment."Kafka 4.3 also documents Share Groups with individual-record acquisition.
"Redis Streams are ephemeral."Durability depends on the configured RDB/AOF/replication policy, not the data type.
"SQS FIFO guarantees exactly-once database/API effects."Deduplication and ordering are scoped queue behaviors, not an atomic external commit.
"SQS is zero ops."Fully managed, but the application still owns IAM, quotas, polling, idempotency, and DLQ.
03 Drill

Wording constraints: rapid-fire avoid vs. use instead

AvoidUse instead
"RabbitMQ/Kafka/Redis/SQS is fastest/best.""For this workload, the routing, replay, ordering, and ownership contract favors X; validate with representative tests."
"Direct Reply-To guarantees a reply.""Direct Reply-To replies are at most once; callers need a timeout and safe retry."
"A queue per user is an anti-pattern.""Assess cardinality, churn, lifecycle, replication cost, isolation, and replay needs."
"DLQ means failed messages are safe.""A DLX is an exchange; destination topology and transfer mode determine retention, loss, and duplicate boundaries."
"Quorum delivery limit stops every retry loop.""In 4.3, basic.nack returns don't advance delivery count; apply an explicit budget."
"Prefetch 1 is reliable and fair.""Prefetch is an in-flight bound; reliability comes from acks and idempotency, and the value is workload-specific."
"Publisher confirm means delivered.""It means RabbitMQ reached the documented publish acceptance boundary — not routing or consumer processing."
"Kafka exactly once" (unscoped)"Kafka transactions/Streams provide scoped guarantees for coordinated Kafka processing; external effects need cooperation or idempotency."
"Redis Streams are durable.""Their durability follows the configured Redis persistence, replication, and failover model."
"SQS FIFO is exactly once.""FIFO suppresses duplicate sends under its documented deduplication rule; external processing stays separately idempotent."
04 Mock interview

Question, weak answer, strengthened answer

A mock exchange — the pattern to reuse for any question in the bankinterview transcript

“Walk me through what happens if a consumer crashes mid-job on a quorum queue.”

Weak answer: “The message gets redelivered because it's a quorum queue, so it's safe.”

This answer conflates two different safety properties — quorum replication and delivery acknowledgement — and stops at the headline claim exactly where the interviewer wants to probe.

Strengthened answer: “The crash matters relative to two independent boundaries. If the consumer crashes before acknowledging, the message is simply unacknowledged and gets redelivered — that's a free redelivery, not a duplicate, because no side effect happened yet. If the consumer had already committed a side effect and crashed before acknowledging, the redelivery is now a genuine duplicate attempt, and only an idempotency key on that side effect makes it safe. The quorum replication is a separate property entirely — it's what keeps the message itself from being lost if the node hosting the queue's leader fails, provided it was already publisher-confirmed to a majority. None of this gives me exactly-once; it gives me at-least-once with a duplicate window I have to close in the consumer.”

The strengthened version names both boundaries, states which failure mode produces a safe redelivery versus a real duplicate, separates queue-level replication from consumer-level acknowledgement, and lands on the same honest guarantee statement this course has used since lesson 43. That's the reusable shape: identify the boundary, name what's on each side of it, then state the honest final guarantee.

Self-test

1. Why is "durable queue + persistent message = no loss" incomplete?

Durability and persistence only cover metadata and message-storage survival across a restart. Confirms, routing/return handling, the queue type's replication model, and client recovery all remain necessary — an unresolved confirm is still a duplicate risk.

2. What's the difference between a consumer acknowledgement and a publisher confirm?

They are orthogonal mechanisms on opposite hops: consumer ack covers broker-to-consumer responsibility, publisher confirm covers publisher-to-broker acceptance. Neither is an end-to-end business receipt.

3. Does the quorum queue's default delivery limit stop every redelivery loop?

No — AMQP 0-9-1 basic.nack returns do not advance the failure-oriented delivery-count in 4.3, so an unbounded nack-and-requeue loop can bypass it. Bound retries explicitly at the application level.

4. Is RabbitMQ's metadata store choice between Mnesia and Khepri still accurate in 4.3?

No. Khepri is the only supported metadata store in 4.3; Mnesia was removed. The canonical metadata landing page's contradictory two-backend text is stale carry-over.

5. Is "RabbitMQ cannot retain or replay data" still a fair criticism?

No. RabbitMQ Streams are replicated, retained, append-only logs with replay by offset — the destructive-queue-only framing predates Streams and no longer applies.

6. What's the one habit that turns a headline claim into a defensible interview answer?

Naming the specific boundary the claim stops at — which hop, which mechanism, which failure mode is and isn't covered — before the interviewer has to ask for it.
