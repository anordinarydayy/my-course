Modelling Queues for a Workload

Queue design is a chain of decisions: read semantics, failure budget, rates, backlog, recovery, overload, and operability. This capstone turns an order workload into explicit estimates, separates immutable declaration identity from mutable policy, and defines a benchmark that can falsify the design.

Original study chapter. The model, scenario, formulas, code, experiments, and SVGs are original. Stable behavior targets open-source RabbitMQ 4.3; runnable code uses Python 3 and Pika 1.3.2. This chapter contains three original figures, two interactive. Capacity formulas are engineering estimates, never RabbitMQ guarantees.
00 Requirements first

Write a failure-and-read contract before choosing a queue

“We need a queue for orders” omits every decision that matters. Is each order destructive work for one worker, or retained history for several readers? Can one node’s permanent storage loss lose accepted work? How long can all consumers be unavailable? May stale work expire, old work be dropped, or new work be rejected? Which producer and consumer evidence will applications act on?

Use this order: (1) work versus replay and required ordering scope; (2) restart, node-loss, majority-loss, and storage-loss budget; (3) temporary versus long-lived topology; (4) publish rate, acknowledged capacity, payload distribution, fan-out, outage horizon, and retention; (5) valid overload victim; (6) confirms, retries, acknowledgements, and idempotency.

Modelling invariant

Select type from semantics and failure budget. Estimate backlog from rates and time. Select overflow from the business victim. Put immutable identity in declarations, mutable supported controls in policies, then benchmark the exact contract under failure.

01 Configuration boundary

Declare identity once; tune supported behavior with policies ▸

Queue name, type, durable, exclusive, and auto_delete define the entity. Queue declarations are idempotent only when required properties and applicable declaration-time arguments are equivalent. A mismatch normally closes the channel with 406 PRECONDITION_FAILED; it does not transform the existing queue.

x-queue-type is immutable. Classic x-max-priority is also declaration-time and cannot be supplied by policy. Changing the default queue type affects only future declarations. RabbitMQ 4.3 rejects legacy x-queue-version=1 and any x-queue-mode instead of converting storage.

Use regular policies for supported operational settings such as message TTL, queue expiration, max length/bytes, overflow, DLX, and several quorum controls. They can change without shipping every application. A policy update can take a few seconds across a busy or large topology, so “dynamic” does not mean instantaneous.

02 Precedence

“Policies always win” and “arguments always win” are both wrong

At most one regular policy applies to a queue: the matching policy with the highest priority. Equal-priority matches are nondeterministic, and regular policies do not merge. Put all required keys for one resource class in a single policy rather than expecting independent policy fragments to combine.

In the general case, client x-arguments override a regular user policy. Operator policies impose guardrails; for numeric resource limits, the lower, more conservative value is generally effective. But precedence is key- and type-specific, not a universal replacement rule. Stable 4.3 documents exceptions: stream configuration values and quorum queue overflow give policy precedence over queue arguments.

Some stream settings are creation-time even when a policy can supply them. x-stream-max-segment-size-bytes and x-stream-filter-size-bytes must be present when the stream is declared; changing the policy later does not reconfigure them. Prefer explicit declaration arguments for those. Stream max-age and max-length-bytes are dynamic retention settings.

Production warning

Before changing a policy, inventory the winning policy, queue arguments, operator policy, queue type, and key-specific precedence. Test the effective value through the management API and behavior; do not infer it from one slogan or from the policy definition alone.

03 Quantitative model

Estimate backlog, logical bytes, and catch-up time ▸

Let λp be sustained publish rate, λc safely acknowledged consumer capacity, T outage or burst duration, and S mean body bytes. Growth while both sides run is max(0, λp − λc) messages/s. A total consumer outage creates approximately B = λp × T ready messages and B × S logical ready-body bytes.

After recovery, catch-up exists only if λc > λp. Net drain is λc − λp, so drain time is B / (λc − λp). Use p95/p99 rates and body sizes too. Fan-out creates independently retained copies. Physical storage exceeds logical bodies through properties, logs, indexes, replication, unacked state, compaction, and free-space headroom; a bare multiplication by replica count is not a safe disk model.

04 Worked scenario

Model a ten-minute outage for correctness-critical orders

An order service publishes 800 messages/s with 2,000-byte mean bodies. A ten-minute complete consumer outage is 600 seconds, producing 800 × 600 = 480,000 ready messages. Logical body volume is 480,000 × 2,000 = 960,000,000 bytes—about 960 MB decimal—before properties, queue internals, replication, unacked deliveries, logs, and safety headroom.

Recovered consumers safely acknowledge 1,000/s while 800/s continue arriving. Net drain is only 200/s, so catch-up takes 480,000 / 200 = 2,400 seconds, or 40 minutes. If acknowledged capacity were 800/s, drain time would be infinite; if 700/s, backlog would continue growing by 100/s.

Orders cannot be silently sacrificed, so drop-head violates the business invariant. A quorum queue with publisher confirms and producer-visible reject-publish is a candidate. A limit slightly above the tested outage envelope might bound ready state, but quorum rejection can overshoot and the byte/count limit is not a disk quota. The producer must fail or back off upstream and retry idempotently.

Interview follow-up

State the arithmetic and its limits: “480,000 messages and 960 MB of bodies are first-pass estimates, not disk sizing. We benchmark confirmed writes, replication, body distribution, consumer delay, and node failure with headroom.”

05 Falsification

Benchmark the contract, not a one-queue happy path

RabbitMQ PerfTest can vary queue type, durability, persistence, confirms, prefetch, producer/consumer rates, body size, queue count, client count, and TLS. A single-queue microbenchmark is not a cluster capacity result: one queue replica’s hot path is limited to one CPU core. Use several queues when production will partition work, and use Stream PerfTest for Stream-protocol performance.

Run steady state, burst, complete consumer outage, recovery, one member loss, leader transition, and producer reconnect. Measure publish and acknowledged rates, ready/unacked depth, oldest age, confirm and end-to-end latency percentiles, CPU, memory, disk I/O/free space, network, redeliveries, nacks, and time to catch up.

For a rough minimum link estimate, RabbitMQ’s production guide uses message rate × message size × 1.10 × 8 bits/s before workload-specific traffic. Replication, fan-out, consumer delivery, TLS, inter-node forwarding, and retries add more. Provision durable low-latency storage and disk headroom because log reclamation is asynchronous.

06 Build

Encode the estimate, then declare only immutable intent

The runnable model calculates the order envelope, rejects an impossible recovery plan, and declares a short-lived lab resource with only immutable type identity, explicitly deleted in finally. In production, a policy—not copied application arguments—would own the tested length/overflow guardrail.

Complete workload checkpointPython 3 · Pika 1.3.2

Make units and assumptions executable

python · install pika==1.3.2 and run against localhost
from dataclasses import dataclass
import uuid

import pika


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Workload:
    publish_per_second: int
    ack_per_second_after_recovery: int
    outage_seconds: int
    mean_body_bytes: int


def model(workload):
    backlog = workload.publish_per_second * workload.outage_seconds
    logical_body_bytes = backlog * workload.mean_body_bytes
    net_drain = (
        workload.ack_per_second_after_recovery
        - workload.publish_per_second
    )
    if net_drain <= 0:
        raise ValueError("consumer capacity must exceed continuing arrivals")
    drain_seconds = backlog / net_drain
    return backlog, logical_body_bytes, net_drain, drain_seconds


orders = Workload(
    publish_per_second=800,
    ack_per_second_after_recovery=1_000,
    outage_seconds=10 * 60,
    mean_body_bytes=2_000,
)
backlog, body_bytes, net_drain, drain_seconds = model(orders)
assert backlog == 480_000
assert body_bytes == 960_000_000
assert net_drain == 200
assert drain_seconds == 2_400

queue = f"lab.orders.work.{uuid.uuid4().hex[:10]}"
connection = pika.BlockingConnection(pika.ConnectionParameters("localhost"))
channel = connection.channel()
try:
    channel.queue_declare(
        queue=queue,
        durable=True,
        exclusive=False,
        auto_delete=False,
        arguments={"x-queue-type": "quorum"},
    )
    print(
        {
            "candidate": "quorum + confirms + reject-publish policy",
            "backlog_messages": backlog,
            "logical_body_bytes": body_bytes,
            "net_drain_per_second": net_drain,
            "drain_seconds": drain_seconds,
        }
    )
finally:
    channel.queue_delete(queue=queue)
    connection.close()

A matching production policy might own max-length and overflow=reject-publish after benchmark evidence chooses the values. It must be one coherent policy with deliberate priority, observed effective settings, and an operator guardrail if the platform team requires one. The 480,000 estimate is a test point—not a default or promised maximum.

Self-test

1. What happens when an application redeclares a quorum queue as classic?

The properties are not equivalent, so RabbitMQ normally closes the channel with 406 PRECONDITION_FAILED; the queue does not change type.

2. Can two matching regular policies merge TTL and DLX keys?

No. At most one regular policy applies: the highest priority match. Equal-priority matches are nondeterministic, so required keys belong together.

3. Do client arguments or policies always win?

Neither slogan is universal. Client arguments generally beat regular policy, operator numeric limits usually take the lower value, and stream/quorum-overflow exceptions are documented.

4. What backlog forms at 800 messages/s during 600 seconds of total outage?

Approximately 480,000 messages: 800 × 600.

5. Why is drain time infinite when recovery capacity equals publish rate?

Net drain is zero. Consumers process new arrivals but remove none of the accumulated backlog.

6. Why is logical body volume not physical disk sizing?

It excludes properties, unacked state, indexes, logs, replication, compaction behavior, segment granularity, and required free-space headroom.
